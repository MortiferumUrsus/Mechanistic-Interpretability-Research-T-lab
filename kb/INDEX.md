# Knowledge Base Index

Built for: training a cheap denoiser $D$ on GPT-2-small (124M, 12 layers) residual-stream activations, trained via $\lVert h - D(h+\epsilon)\rVert^2$ (or a $t\cdot h+(1-t)\cdot\epsilon$ interpolation), applied at inference as $\tilde h = D(h+\alpha v)$ after the middle layer to recover fluency lost to large-alpha activation steering, evaluated as a fluency-vs-concept-presence Pareto front.

All PDFs are in `kb/pdf/`. All notes are in `kb/notes/`.

| Slug | Citation | Why it matters here | Single most actionable technique |
|---|---|---|---|
| [`glp-generative-latent-prior`](notes/glp-generative-latent-prior.md) | Luo, Feng, Darrell, Radford, Steinhardt, "Learning a Generative Meta-Model of LLM Activations" (GLP), arXiv:2602.06964, ICML 2026 | **THE key baseline** — trains a diffusion/flow-matching denoiser over residual activations and uses it to project steered (off-manifold) activations back onto the manifold, evaluated as exactly the fluency-vs-concept Pareto front the task wants | The SDEdit-style partial-denoising trick: don't denoise from pure noise — initialize the reverse process directly from the steered activation `acts+alpha*v` at an intermediate noise level `t_start` (they use `t_start=0.5, num_steps=20`), and treat `t_start` as a tunable knob for "how much to trust the denoiser vs. the raw steered input" |
| [`persona-vectors`](notes/persona-vectors.md) | Chen, Arditi, Sleight, Evans, Lindsey, "Persona Vectors: Monitoring and Controlling Character Traits in Language Models," arXiv:2507.21509 | Source of the exact steering equation ($h_\ell \leftarrow h_\ell + \alpha v_\ell$), a validated difference-of-means vector-extraction recipe, and the paired concept+coherence LLM-judge eval pattern to copy for the Pareto front's y/x axes | Extract/steer using **response-token** activations, not prompt-token activations (empirically more effective — their footnote 2), and filter your contrastive extraction set by both trait-score AND coherence-score ≥50 before computing the mean-difference direction |
| [`persona-vectors-judge-prompts`](notes/persona-vectors-judge-prompts.md) | (same repo/paper as above) — verbatim prompts from `github.com/safety-research/persona_vectors` | Ready-to-reuse, verbatim LLM-judge prompt templates for a 0–100 concept-expression score and a 0–100 coherence (fluency) score, plus the meta-template used to auto-generate new trait-judges | Reuse the **coherence_0_100** prompt verbatim as your fluency-axis LLM judge, and implement scoring via the logprob-weighted-average-over-valid-integers trick (`max_tokens=1, top_logprobs=20`) instead of parsing free-text numbers |
| [`saelens`](notes/saelens.md) | Bloom, Tigges, Duong, Chanin, SAELens (software), `github.com/decoderesearch/SAELens` | Source of the actual GPT-2-small SAE decoder columns (`gpt2-small-res-jb` release) you'd use as steering vectors $v$, with exact hook names, `d_sae`, and code | `sae.W_dec[feature_idx]` (shape `[24576, 768]` rows) **is** your ready-made steering direction; note the release only has `hook_resid_pre` per layer (use the **layer-7** SAE, `blocks.7.hook_resid_pre`, to match "after middle-layer-6" = `blocks.6.hook_resid_post`, since those are the same tensor) |
| [`transformerlens`](notes/transformerlens.md) | Nanda et al., TransformerLens, `github.com/TransformerLensOrg/TransformerLens` | Defines the hook-name convention (`blocks.{L}.hook_resid_post`, etc.) and the mechanics for splicing a custom function (e.g. your denoiser) into `model.generate`'s decoding loop | Wrap `model.generate(...)` in `with model.hooks(fwd_hooks=[(name, fn)]):` — **not** `run_with_hooks`, which only runs a single forward pass and silently won't apply across `generate`'s autoregressive loop |
| [`actadd-turner2023`](notes/actadd-turner2023.md) | Turner et al., "Steering Language Models With Activation Engineering," arXiv:2308.10248 | Originates the simplest additive-steering baseline and is one of the first papers to explicitly quantify fluency degradation from steering (reports 1.5–3x perplexity penalty) | Middle-layer injection is empirically most effective on GPT-2-XL — corroborates the task's "after the middle layer" design; always pair a concept-strength metric with a prompt-relevance metric (cosine sim) so large alpha doesn't just make the model ignore the prompt while still scoring "high concept" |
| [`caa-rimsky2023`](notes/caa-rimsky2023.md) | Rimsky et al., "Steering Llama 2 via Contrastive Activation Addition," arXiv:2312.06681, ACL 2024 | Generalizes ActAdd to many-pair mean-difference vectors with a systematic per-layer sweep and an MMLU-based capability-preservation check | Average the contrast-pair difference over many pairs (not one) to reduce direction noise — cheap (just more forward passes) and directly portable to a GPT-2-small setup |
| [`norm-preserving-steering-fluency`](notes/norm-preserving-steering-fluency.md) | Aparin & Gaintseva, arXiv:2606.06735; Nguyen et al., arXiv:2605.01167; (context: arXiv:2501.09929) | Explains the *mechanism* of fluency degradation (uncontrolled norm/radial change, and "collateral damage" to non-target directions under a naive isotropic assumption) and gives training-free baselines to compare your learned denoiser against | Log $\lVert h\rVert$, $\lVert h+\alpha v\rVert$, and $\lVert D(h+\alpha v)\rVert$ separately from angular/cosine shift — this tells you whether your denoiser's fluency recovery is mostly a norm-correction effect, which is cheap to verify and to replicate with a training-free covariance-aware projection baseline |
| [`distinct-n-li2016`](notes/distinct-n-li2016.md) | Li, Galley, Brockett, Gao, Dolan, "A Diversity-Promoting Objective Function for Neural Conversation Models," NAACL 2016 / arXiv:1510.03055 | Original, exact definition of distinct-1/2/(3) — the dist-n diversity/fluency-proxy metric named in the task's x-axis | distinct-$n$ = (unique $n$-grams in the **pooled, corpus-level** set of generations) / (**total token count**) — compute corpus-level and token-normalized, not per-sentence-averaged, and always hold generation length fixed across alpha conditions before comparing |
| [`concept-fluency-pareto-eval-practice`](notes/concept-fluency-pareto-eval-practice.md) | Synthesis of arXiv:2505.20063 (SAEs Are Good for Steering), arXiv:2501.09929 (FGAA), + cross-reference to GLP | Standard-practice survey for how the field actually builds the (fluency, concept) Pareto front you need, plus a synthesized recommendation combining perplexity + distinct-n + LLM coherence judge on the x-axis | Sample your alpha grid densely near the empirically-reported inflection point (~scale 40 in one paper's units, but re-derive your own on GPT-2 small) rather than uniformly — steering-induced fluency collapse is a threshold effect, not a smooth curve |
| [`openai-sparse-autoencoder`](notes/openai-sparse-autoencoder.md) | Gao, Dupré la Tour, Tillman, Goh, Troll, Radford, Sutskever, Leike, Wu, "Scaling and Evaluating Sparse Autoencoders," arXiv:2406.04093 + `github.com/openai/sparse_autoencoder` | Alternative source of GPT-2-small SAEs (TopK/exact-sparsity architecture, ReLU+L1 older variant also available), useful if you want an exact-L0 steering-vector source instead of SAELens's empirical-L0 release | Prefer the `v5_32k`/`v5_128k` TopK releases over `v1`/`v4` ReLU+L1 if you want a precisely-known, exact number of active latents per token rather than an empirical average |

## Suggested reading order
1. `glp-generative-latent-prior.md` — read first, it is the closest existing solution to the exact task.
2. `persona-vectors.md` + `persona-vectors-judge-prompts.md` — steering equation + reusable judge prompts.
3. `saelens.md` and/or `openai-sparse-autoencoder.md` — pick a concrete source for steering vectors $v$.
4. `transformerlens.md` — implementation mechanics for hooking GPT-2 small.
5. `actadd-turner2023.md`, `caa-rimsky2023.md`, `norm-preserving-steering-fluency.md` — establish the "why steering breaks fluency" baseline and alternative training-free fixes to benchmark against.
6. `distinct-n-li2016.md` + `concept-fluency-pareto-eval-practice.md` — finalize your exact eval protocol.
